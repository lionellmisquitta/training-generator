# Curated academic starting points (verified web discovery 2026-10-25)

Use these as research leads, not as licenses to copy lecture text. Reopen links and validate individual materials when authoring lessons. Cite specific lecture, assignment, or paper used. Separate primary research claims from discussion and inference.

- Stanford CS336 (Spring 2026), https://cs336.stanford.edu/ — tokenizer, Transformer training from scratch, GPU optimization, scaling, data, alignment. Assignments and lecture materials. Strong choice for implementation depth.
- Stanford CS224N (Winter 2026), https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224n/ — word vectors, tensor gradients, self-attention, Transformers, LLM benchmarking. Some current video access restricted to enrolled students; public past recordings linked.
- Carnegie Mellon 11-785 (Fall 2026), https://deeplearning.cs.cmu.edu/ — neural network fundamentals and practical implementations; HW3 covers attention from scratch in NumPy.
- UC Berkeley CS285, https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse/ — deep reinforcement learning; useful for advanced alignment/agent learning once prerequisites established.
- MIT 6.S191, https://introtodeeplearning.com/ — introductory deep-learning lecture materials; use as prerequisite refresh, not as graduate-level ceiling.
- Primary research: Vaswani et al. (2017) https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762 and Dao et al. FlashAttention (2022) https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135.
- Hacker News official API https://github.com/HackerNews/API and search https://hn.algolia.com/api — discover engineering anecdotes, disagreement, and practical failures; never treat comments as primary evidence.

Research method: concept -> prerequisites -> primary academic sources -> equations/implementation -> competing claims -> original worked derivation -> reproducible lab -> source-specific reading links. Label materials that are suggested but not actually examined. Never reproduce copyrighted course notes wholesale.
